Video Moment Retrieval (VMR) aims to identify the temporal segments of a video that correspond to a given natural language query. The field has developed rapidly with the introduction of Transformer-based architectures, vision-language models, and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, existing surveys often focus on specific stages of this development and provide limited coverage of emerging tasks such as Video Corpus Moment Retrieval (VCMR) and spatio-temporal video grounding.

This survey provides a unified review of VMR methods and organises existing approaches across five dimensions: era and architectural paradigm, MLLM functional role, MLLM training paradigm, data perspective, and task scope. It also reviews the development of model architectures, feature representations, datasets, evaluation metrics, and different task settings.

In addition, the survey examines biases in commonly used VMR benchmarks and discusses the challenges of fair and reliable evaluation. An evaluation audit of recent MLLM-based methods is presented to investigate whether current approaches have been tested using bias-aware benchmark settings. The survey also examines the transition from traditional VMR approaches to MLLM-based methods and highlights the growing role of multimodal language models in video-language understanding.

Finally, several open challenges are identified, including long-video grounding, corpus-level retrieval, non-text queries, spatio-temporal grounding, and reliable evaluation on bias-aware benchmarks. These findings provide a structured view of the current VMR landscape and highlight directions for future research in video-language understanding.
